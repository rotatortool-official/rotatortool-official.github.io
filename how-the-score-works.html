How the Rotator score works

Every 15 minutes, about 250 Binance coins and tokenized stocks each get one number, from −50 to 100. Scroll, and the coin comes apart into the pieces that make it.

One number, four parts

Momentum and the comparison with the big markets say how the coin has moved. Supply says what could weigh on it: big unlocks and high inflation pull the score down, and they are the reason a score can go below zero. The size nudge is a small, fixed step by market cap.

A high score means the coin has been stronger than the rest. A low score means it has been weaker.

What the score is not

It describes what already happened. It is not a forecast. Across 771 days of history, moving from a high-scoring coin into a low one did not beat picking two coins at random, so Rotator shows the score as information, not as a call. The track record shows every result, good or bad.

The numbers on the coin are an example, not a live coin. Live scores are on the board.
